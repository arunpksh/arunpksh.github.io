Born in the UNESCO World Heritage temple town of Belur in Karnataka, India, I grew up in the IT capital city of Bengaluru. I studied Mechanical Engineering at B.M.S. College of Engineering and subsequently spent about a year and a half working in the software industry before returning to engineering and pursuing my interest in computational mechanics.

In 2003, I moved to Stuttgart, Germany, to join the Computational Mechanics of Materials and Structures (COMMAS) program at the University of Stuttgart. I graduated in 2005, having developed a particular interest in the mechanics of materials, which also formed the focus of my Master's thesis.

In September 2005, I joined the Fraunhofer Institute for Mechanics of Materials IWM in Freiburg, Germany, where I began my research in Computational Materials Science. My Ph.D. work focused primarily on the micromechanics of polycrystalline materials, including the computational modeling of complex material behavior such as twinning and recrystallization. I successfully defended my doctoral thesis at the Karlsruhe Institute of Technology (KIT) in December 2009 under the supervision of Prof. Dr. Hermann Riedel and Prof. Dr. Peter Gumbsch. Subsequently, I worked as a postdoctoral fellow at the Fraunhofer IWM in Freiburg, where I worked mainly on industry projects related to process modeling while also extending my Ph.D. research.

In October 2011, I moved to Nuremberg, Germany, and joined the Institute-I Materials Science and Engineering at Friedrich-Alexander-Universität Erlangen-Nürnberg (FAU), where I worked in the group of Prof. Erik Bitzek. A particular focus of my work at FAU was the development and application of computational frameworks for materials processing and microstructure evolution. This included the development of FE2AT, a framework linking finite-element-based deformation with atomistic simulations, as well as a multiscale framework coupling the viscoplastic self-consistent model with finite elements to model the accumulative roll bonding (ARB) process. The latter framework enabled the simulation of texture evolution and anisotropy over multiple ARB passes.

In parallel, I worked extensively on atomistic modeling of deformation and microstructural behavior at the nanoscale, with a particular interest in interface-dominated materials. My research included the development of methods for generating realistic atomistic microstructures, investigations of irradiation and focused-ion-beam-induced damage, and studies of deformation mechanisms in nanocrystalline materials and Ni-based superalloys.

During my time at FAU, I was also actively involved in teaching and student supervision in the fields of materials science and computational materials modeling. My teaching activities included Angewandte Grundlagen der Werkstoffwissenschaften (V+Ü, 4 SWS), Materials Properties (V, 2 SWS), Introduction to the Finite Element Method (V, 2 SWS), Rechneranwendungen in der Werkstoffwissenschaften (V+Ü, 4 SWS), Computational Nanoscience (Ü, 2 SWS), and a Hauptseminar conducted in English (2 SWS). I taught several of these courses together with Prof. Erik Bitzek and other colleagues, including Dr. S. Neumeier and Dr. T. Fey, and co-organized the English-language Hauptseminar with Prof. P. Felfer. In addition, I supervised Master's theses and student projects, providing students with guidance in computational mechanics, materials modeling, and numerical simulation.

I was also involved in the academic administration of the Elite International Master’s Program in Advanced Materials and Processes (MAP) at FAU. As a member of the Studienkommission of the program, I contributed to its academic governance and also served on the selection committee responsible for interviewing prospective candidates.

In April 2018, I joined the Chair of Micromechanical Materials Modeling at TU Bergakademie Freiberg as a Senior Scientist, working with Prof. Stefan Sandfeld. My research during this period built on my previous work in multiscale and atomistic materials modeling, while increasingly incorporating concepts from materials informatics and data science. A central focus was the development of machine learning (ML) frameworks to extract physically meaningful information from complex, large-scale simulation data. This included the development of OrISODATA, an orientation-based ISODATA clustering approach for automated grain segmentation in atomistic simulations, as well as a statistical ML framework for understanding strain partitioning in polycrystalline materials. These activities complemented my continued work on multiscale modeling frameworks, atomistic modeling, method development, and open-source software. This included the development of solution-mapping and remeshing approaches for crystal plasticity simulations and OptiMic, a tool for generating optimized polycrystalline microstructures for finite-element and atomistic simulations.

Since 2022, I have been part of the newly formed Micro-Mechanics & Multiscale Materials Modeling (M⁵) Group at TUBAF, working with Prof. Bernhard Eidel. My work during this period has covered a range of materials and phenomena, including nanoparticle sintering, severe plastic deformation, metallic and oxide materials, grain-boundary phenomena, and nanoscale plasticity. These activities have increasingly brought together atomistic modeling, computational materials science, and data-driven analysis to investigate the mechanisms linking processing, microstructure, and material behavior.

In November 2025, I successfully defended my Habilitation thesis, entitled “Understanding Material Behavior at the Nanoscale via Atomistic Simulations, Data Informatics and Machine Learning.” In 2026, I received Venia Legendi, the authorization to teach independently in the field of Computational Materials Science (Rechnergestützte Materialwissenschaft).

My teaching activities at TU Bergakademie Freiberg are primarily within the Computational Materials Science (CMS) Master's program, where I contribute to courses covering software tools and programming, atomistic simulation methods, crystal plasticity, texture and anisotropy, and the theory, modeling, and simulation of microstructures. In addition to teaching, I have supervised numerous Master's theses and Personal Programming Projects (PPP), supporting students in developing their computational, programming, and simulation skills and applying them to problems in computational materials science.

In addition to my research and teaching activities, I contribute to the scientific community through the development and maintenance of open-source software for computational materials science, including OptiMic, nanoSCULPT, FE2AT, IdentiPy, and ParIdent-VPSC. I regularly serve as a referee for international journals in materials science, computational mechanics, and computational materials science. I have also contributed to the organization of international symposia, workshops, and scientific meetings, and have delivered invited tutorials on topics including atomistic simulations, finite element modeling, and computational materials science. Through these activities, I aim to contribute to the development, dissemination, and application of computational methods within the materials science community.
